Choose Your Cosmos

Play With a Friend

Two katamaris in one world. You can see each other rolling around, and anything either of you picks up is gone for both of you. Nobody can roll anybody else up — there is no way to get in each other's way at all.

Worth knowing first. This connects your device straight to your friend's, the same way a video call does — so each of you can see the other's IP address. There is no server holding the game, no account, no sign-in, and nothing is written down anywhere. Only send an invite to someone you would give your phone number to.

Or join with a room code:

Pick the one you will both roll. Your friend does not choose — the invite carries it, and their game loads the same world from it.

How to Play

Tilt ↕
Tip the top of the phone away from you to roll forward, and back toward you to reverse. It is a throttle, not a switch — a gentle tip crawls, a firm one runs
Tilt ↔
Roll the phone like a steering wheel to swing the camera. The katamari always rolls the way the camera faces, so this is how you steer
DASH
Hold for a burst of speed. You need it to climb — a slope you crawl at will stop you. The bar inside the button is how much dash you have left
SUPER
A gold rim fills around the DASH button as you roll without using it. Let it fill all the way and the button glows — your next dash then lasts five times as long. Spend it on a straight run through a crowd of things
RECENTRE
Re-zero both tilts to however you are holding the phone now. Use it whenever you shift position
✕
Hold to quit back to the title. It fills up as you hold
II
Pause. From there you can restart or quit

Prefer buttons? Options → Turning swaps the steering for on-screen ‹ › arrows, and Tilt range sets how far you have to tip for full speed.

No motion sensor, or you declined the permission? On-screen ▲ and ▼ arrows appear in place of RECENTRE and drive the katamari instead, and the ‹ › arrows come back to steer with. Everything else is identical.

Anything smaller than your katamari sticks to it. Every object you collect makes the ball bigger, which makes bigger things collectable. Long thin things can be picked up even when they look too big — they will make you roll strangely until you cover them up. Slamming into something huge at speed will shake objects loose.

The stage does not end when you hit the goal — it ends when the clock does, and the size you finish at is the size that counts. Once you have passed the goal you can press Enter to stop early and bank what you have. If you manage to roll up absolutely everything, the round closes itself.
